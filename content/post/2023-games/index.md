I've played a lot of games in 2023!  In an effort to try and write more I thought I'd try and write a short review for each of them.  Warning: Any of these reviews contain plot spoilers, so please skip any that you're still interested in playing!

## Final Fantasy XIV Online: Endwalker

{{< figure src="games/endwalker.png" >}}

TL;DR:  Not quite as good as the other expansions' post-launch content, but still very fun to play.

2023 was the second year of Endwalker and brought to a close most of the story arcs we've seen this year.  Overall I think the expansion content has been pretty good, although I do share the majority opinion that Shadowbringers had significantly stronger post-expansion patches.

The MSQ was very fine.  I think Zero is maybe not the most interesting character to focus a story around, and she was extremely cliched, but this is also a JRPG, a genre built on tropes, so I think one does go in with specific expectations. The story that came out with the 6.3 and 6.4 patches, both of which released this year, were probably more boring than normal, but 6.5 brought it to a decent enough conclusion that in retrospect will make this a decent enough story.  I heard someone describe this as "A trial series that was padded to make an MSQ story" and I think that's a pretty accurate assessment of the post-endwalker MSQ.

The raids were definitely the highlight of the expansion.  The Myths of the Realm alliance raid were just the correct amount of not-serious you need for 24-man content.  The raids were a bit too easy (and they do have an item-level scaling problem) but overall they were very fun and I don't have any big complaints.  

Pandaemonium, the normal raid, was probably the best part of Endwalker in 2023.  The ending of the story was great, and the four raid teirs were probably the best wing they've ever released.  There's usually at least one fight per tier that I'm not excited to see, but they're all fun this time (although I will say that spider-building is my least favorite).

The rest of Endwalker is not very noteworthy.  The Spendorous Tools were fun to make, but nothing to write home about.  The Manderville story is great, as usual, but the Manderville relic weapons were definitely a real letdown.  Eureka Orthos and the Variant Dungeons were both incredibly fun, but also content that I run once and then never touch again.

Overall, post-Endwalker didn't live up to the heights of post-Shadowbringers or post-Stormblood, but those are big shoes to fill and I thought post-Endwalker was still fun even if it doesn't take the number 1 spot.

## It Takes Two

{{< figure src="games/it takes two.png" >}}

TL;DR:  While still not perfect, it's one of the best games in its genre.

It Takes Two is a two-player co-op game by the author or A Way Out (and one of the creaters of Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons).  I loved Brothers, and I thought A Way Out was flawed, but still a pretty fun game, so I was excited to try this one out.  Plus, there aren't a ton of games that I'm interested in playing with my spouse, so when an acclaimed two-player co-op game shows up, I have to give it a shot.

This game is really good, and an improvement on A Way Out in basically every way.  The puzzles are less frustrating, but can still sometimes give that eureka moment when you figure out the correct way to do it.  Their game engine and models can lead to a lot of uncanny valley, but the emphasis on a more cartoony art style for the main characters alievaites a lot of that.

I also really appreciated the little mini games scattered around the world.  They were an excellent reward for stopping and exploring your environment rather than speeding forward, and a great way to break up the game from becoming too much of the same thing all the time.

My only real complaint of the game is that I felt it went on a bit long.  Steam clocked my playtime at 11 hours, which is a great value, but I did feel like I was done with it maybe an hour or hour and a half before the game actually finished.

## Horizon Zero Dawn: Complete Edition

{{< figure src="games/horizon zero dawn.png" >}}

TL;DR:  If you like Ubisoft-style games, this one is one of the best in its class.

Horizon Zero Dawn feels so much like an Ubisoft game, that I had to stop in the middle of playing it to look up who made it (Spoilers, it's not Ubisoft).  It adheres almost painfully to the formula, with unlockable spots around a map revealing the fog and plopping down a series of side quests.

For me, these games fall into two categories:  Games where I find the core-gameplay to be fun and engaging and I typically come close to 100%-ing all of the game's content, or games were I find the side-content to be mind numbingly dull and I tend to just play through the main content of the game.  I don't have a problem with either kind of game, but Horizon fell into the former category, which meant that I got a TON of playtime out of this one.  I think I did every piece of side content except some of the hardest challenges provided by the DLC content.

The world of Horizon Zero Dawn was quite engaging.  While the robot dinosaurs weren't mostly a novelty, they fact that they were robotic lead to a fun "shoot the weak point" design, and it was fun to set up traps for specific dinos when you had a specific hunt mission.  The various tribes and communities were also fun, and made for a neat post-apocolyptic world.

The story was nothing to write home about.  There weren't any big twists that you couldn't figure out from a mile away, but it was still a fun ride.

The biggest letdown in the game was the crafting system, and that's typically just something that I don't really like in a game. Stopping to try and hunt down 7 rabbit skins was more of a pain than anything else, and I would've preferred it if the game just had material upgrades that you buy with loot or experience.

Oeverall, a great game if you like the formula, and I'm excited to play the sequel in the future.

## Wandersong

{{< figure src="games/wandersong.png" >}}

TL;DR:  Great story, wholesome dialog, two thumbs way up.  Go play this (and also Chicory)

Ever since I played Chicory: A Colorful Tale last year, I've been interested in playing Greg Lobanov's other game, Wandersong.  I was originally a bit put off by the art-style, the simple polygon shapes look amateurish at first glance next to the more refined art in Chicory.  However, it quickly became apparent that the cardboard cutout choice is as much an artistic choice as it is a convienence, and I quickly started to like it.

I don't know what it is about the writing in these two games but the wholesomeness of it resonates with me. I love the characters, I love the story, and I love the world.

The biggest letdown of Wandersong is that the puzzle/platforming mechanics in the game aren't always super interesting (something that was fixed in Chicory). But, overall this is an extremely strong showing from an early game by a small team.  I've already backed Beastieball on Kickstarter, so I can't wait to see what these developers come up with next.

## Hades

{{< figure src="games/hades.png" >}}

TL;DR: It turns out that one of the most critically acclaimed Indie games to ever release is pretty good, who knew?

After being enamored with Bastion and Transistor, I was excited to play Hades back when it first launched.  Unfortunately, it didn't quite stick with me.  I didn't like the darkness upgrade system...I typically prefer roguelikes where I get farther because my own skill level increases, not because my character gets more powerful, essentially lowering the difficulty.  I was focusing on trying to get my first clear, and after getting only a single clear in 30-odd runs, I put the game aside and played other things. 

This time, when I went back to it, I decided this time that the game was balanced around the darkness system, and that when given the choice, I should first focus on gathering darkness rather than getting the boons and items I needed to clear.  I have to say, this was a significantly more successful strategy, and I got a ton of clears (including an 11-clear streak, with escalating difficulty each time).  Something clicked with me this time over last time, and according to steam I have 146 hours in this game to date.  I cleared through the epilogue and have most of the upgrades in the game cleared before I finally decided to put the game down to play other things.

There's not really any downsides I have with Hades.  The design is well thought out and all the details are incredibly polished (especially the sheer volume of writing...you'll get quite far into the game before you start seeing repated dialog).  If you have any interest in a roguelike brawler, you owe it to yourself to play this one.

## Haven Moon

{{< figure src="games/haven moon.png" >}}

TL;DR: A short but fun Mystlike game.  Easily worth the cost.

This game is in its most literal sense a love letter to the Myst game series (in that there's literally a letter by from the game developer in the game saying "I love Myst games and wanted to make my own").  The game is quite short, steam says 2.4 hours for me, but it does a fantastic job of capturing a sense of wonder and exploration in that short time.  While I did look at a walkthrough a couple of times, there was only one puzzle that I thought was poorly designed (and the puzzle itself was fine, it was simply too difficult to find...you get stuck because you don't know the next puzzle you need to solve even exists).  Otherwise if you played Myst, you know what to expect with this.  If you haven't played Myst, stop and go play one of the all time great games of history.

## Nioh:  Complete Edition

{{< figure src="games/nioh.png" >}}

TL;DR:  DNF, I think I prefer a more soulslike experience over whatever Nioh was offering.

Going into this, I knew this game wasn't as well recieved as Nioh 2, but I like to start games at the beginning at the series if possible.  Unfortunately this one was a miss for me on all levels.

The loot system was a complete turn off.  I'm not a big fan of random stats on random gear, and with there being a LOT of stats in this game, it can be overwhelming to a casual player to myself in figuring out what I care about and what I don't.  The combat stances were...interesting but ultimately not something I enjoyed either. I also felt like the level design lacked polish as well?  I don't remember a lot of details but I found a lot of the levels and enemy layout to be frustrating rather than challenging.

Story-wise I barely got into it.  It felt a bit white-saviory at the beginning which was a bit of a turn off, but I don't know where it leads so I don't feel like I can accurately judge it.

Overall, I don't think it's necessarily a bad game, but one that missed my personal preferences on almost every level.  Give it a shot yourself if it interests you, but it's unlikely that I'll pick up this game or its sequel again.

## Psychonauts 2

{{< figure src="games/psychonauts2.png" >}}

TL;DR: It's more Psychonauts.  They're both amazing 2000s-esque platformers.

Psychonauts 2 is a game that has no right to be as good as it is.  It came out 16 years after the original after a decently long development cycle, so I think most were skeptical of whether this game was going to be any good or not.  As it turns out Double Fine just took the beloved original game, took everything that was great out of it, and then just did more of that.

The "hub" of the game, the Motherlobe (and surrounding area) is much more interesting than the Whispering Rock Summer Camp.  There are collectables and secrets hidden around every corner, and it's easy to spend as much time explorin the hub world for goodies as you do the individual game levels.  The levels themselves are equally well designed, with nothing as poorly designed as the Meat Circus.  Several levels are all-time greats, with the PSI King's Sensorium rivaling the Milkman Conspiracy for best Psychonauts level.

The story is fine...while I won't be thinking about the implications of the plot late at night or anything, it was a definite improvement over the first game, and I enjoyed the addition of the Psychic Seven and Raz's family to the plot.  

The game also made an excellent design choice of giving your all your abilities from the first game right away.  I think the trope of depowering the character at the start of every sequel can be a bit overdone, and I appreciated that Psychonauts 2 just kept all your abilities and just gave you more on top of them.

The weakest part of the game was definitely the menuing.  You can have 4 abilities active at any time, but I felt like I was always pausing the game (even mid combat) to stop and pull out the relevant ability for a situation, which needlessly broke up the flow of the game. I think this was exasterbated by the number of enemies that required a specific ability to defeat, which also lead to late game combat feeling a bit gamey.

Overall though, a fantastic edition to the series.

## Bravely Default

{{< figure src="games/bravely default.png" >}}

TL;DR: DNF. A great start with great mechanics that devolves into a mindless slog halfway through.

Bravely Default is basically impossible to talk about without involving spoilers.  While the other reviews in this post are spoiler-light, I need to talk about the big twist here.  You have been warned!

The first half of Bravely Default is a pretty standard JRPG. The main characters are quite tropey but also quite interesting. You travel around a world in decline with your fairy companion, activating 4 big crystals, fighting a bunch of bad guys dead-set on stopping you.

The game during this phase is quite fun.  The Bravely/Default system which allows you to defer or take extra combat actions is quite good and adds a lot of combat depth.  Additionally, the game has a job system similar to Final Fantasy Tactics, allowing you to mix and match jobs to create powerful characters.

After you activate the 4th crystal, you get sucked into a big vortex and end up in an alternate reality back at the start of the game, a world where everything needs to be done again but slightly different than the first time.  At this point, the game becomes a boss rush.  You can fight the 4 crystal bosses again, and the rest of the bosses become "optional" fights as well.  Everything is harder the second time through and the bosses have new tricks up their sleeve.  Clearing the bosses again, resets the world another time and putting you in a third alternate reality.

It's at this point the game becomes mindlessly boring.  To make sure I wasn't infinitely looping I stopped and looked up a walkthrough at this point.  It turns out you can break the loop at any point but that gets you the "bad" ending.  To get the "good" ending, you need to play through the boss rush an additional 4 times, a feat that will undoubtedly involve a lot of grinding of job points to create overpowered combinations targeted to every enemy's weakness.  If you're in the game for the turn-based combat gameplay, this is probably the highlight of the game for you, as it can get very tough and requires a lot of throught and preparation.  If you're in the game for the story however, this is where the game falls off a cliff, as there's basically no additional plot, just a ton of fighting and grinding.

I put the game down at this point.  I enjoyed my time with it immensely, but the long grind didn't do it for me this time and I was happy to move on to other things.

## Prodigal

{{< figure src="games/prodigal.png" >}}

TL;DR:  DNF (kind of?). Zelda-like with some relationship builing elements.  Good but not great, definitely worth picking up still.

Prodigal took me two goes at to get through.  I tried it early on in the year, and I think I wasn't quite in the mood for it, ended up putting it down for 6 months and then took another go at it.

It's a decent game, and if you want more 2D Legend of Zelda it's worth picking up.  The dungeon design rivals any Legend of Zelda dungeon, and I appreciate that your abilities and items are unique to this game, rather than just ripping off the hookshot, the bow, etc.  If there's one complaint that I have, it's that I didn't feel like the controls were incredibly precice, which lead to some frustration when navigating near pits and other instant kill zones.

The world is pretty great.  The story involves the protagonist returning to his hometown after having left years ago under negative circumstances.  There's a lot of history between him and the townspeople, and unravelling the history of what happened and making amends makes for an interesting narriative.  There is a relationship building aspect: while you can date most of the women in the town, the game pushes you towards one, which I ultimately went with.  I apparently like safe relationships in my video games.

After the credits roll, there's still another half of the game to go, apparently released as post-launch content.  I ended up putting the game down here--after reaching on particularly frustrating dexterity puzzle, I decided I had my money's worth and moved on.  While it's not one of my top games, I've added the other games from this developer to my wish list and would still suggest picking this up.

## DOOM Eternal

{{< figure src="games/doom eternal.png" >}}

TL;DR:  Not quite as good as DOOM (2016) but I still got to kick demon ass and I can't ask for more.

With the exception of Doom 3, you go into a Doom game knowing what you're going to get: you arrive in a level and then you shoot demons in the face until the game tells you you're done.  In that aspect, the game was very successful.

DOOM Eternal also added a lot of movement tech to the game over DOOM 2016, all of which made for great gameplay.  Being able to double jump, air dash, grab the boost poles and continue shooting the entire time you did it made for marvelously fluid gameplay.  It's not Tribes level of smooth, but it's some of the best.

On the other hand, some of the enemies added to the game didn't quite do it for me.  There were a few enemies that required specific strategies to defeat (such as the guy that would block everything execpt during certain times), and mixing them into combat often felt like I had to stop dealing with shooting demons just to deal with this one guy before I could continue again.  On the other hand, adding shootable weak points to many of the enemies without making combat revolve around these weaknesses felt like a positive addition, adding a strategic facet to combat while still allowing me to shotgun demons in the face if strategy didn't pan out.

Unlike DOOM 2016, DOOM Eternal had plot.  I imagine the plot of the game is probably derisive to some, especially since's 2016's bare bone's story was one of the selling points, but I quite liked the story in Eternal.  It's a little bit less ignorable, but it's spaced out enough that I imagine people who just want to skip cutscenes and kill demons will probably continue to get by.

Overall, a great addition to the series.  I think 2016 was maybe a smidge better but more of something I love is always welcome.

## Battle Chef Brigade Deluxe

{{< figure src="games/battle chef.png" >}}

TL;DR: Underrated (slash basically unknown) gem.

Battle Chef Brigade is an action puzzle game about being an Iron Chef in a world where food is made of monsters.  Each round is given a secret ingredient, and personal preferences for each chef, and then you're forced to alternate between killing monsters in a 2D brawler, and then cooking their remains into delishious dishes in a match 3 puzzler, all before time runs out.

Both aspects to the game are fairly simple but together combined with a number of upgrades and an adjustable loadout makes for a good amount of complexity.  The game also does a good job of starting you simple, and gradually introducing you do additional mechanics over the course of the campaign.  The story is fun and well told, filled with a number of memorable characters that you will love.

Overall a great game that I frankly have never seen any discussion about.

## The Sexy Brutale

{{< figure src="games/the sexy brutale.png" >}}

TL;DR: Decent for fans of mystery games...but it never excels.

The Sexy Brutale is a terribly named puzzle game.  You are put into a 12 hour time loop, in which various people in the mansion are killed.  One at a time, you have to observe what leads to a party goer's death, and try to change events to ensure the victim survives.  You may not actually interact with any people, instead you manipulate the environment to achieve your goals (for example, in the tutorial, you replace the bullets in a gun with blanks, preventing someone from being shot to death).  

Taken as a series of individual puzzles, the game is pretty great.  Some of these are eaiser or more inventive than others, but none stand out as either great or terrible. Taken as a whole, the game does feel like it's missing something.  It feels like the game wants to construct events to create one magical day in which you save everyone, but it quickly becomes apparent that this is impossible, and once you save a guest, you will never need to care to save them again.

Overall, good but not great.  Still worth picking up for fans of mystery games.

## The Legend of Heroes: Trails in the Sky SC

{{< figure src="games/trails in the sky sc.png" >}}

TL;DR: It's one of the all time great JRPG series for a reason.

The sequel to the first Trails game, this is essentially just more of the same.  The orbment system is great and leads to a lot of character choice.  The pseudo-tactics combat is unique and provides an extra layer to combat.  

Mostly, however, what makes the Trails games great is the story.  Like the first game, there's definitely a lot of anime tropes at play.  This is especially evident with the villains, who are mustache twirly to stereotypical proportions.  What makes the game great however is the characters.  You've already seen this cast of characters for one game, and they had a lot of character growth, so being able to continue to grow those characters through an entirely second JRPG is fantastic.  And I am a sucker for wholesome writing and this game delivers it in spades.

Also, the game has more Olivier, which instantly makes it a 10/10 in my book.

## The Legend of Zelda:  Oracle of Ages/Seasons

{{< figure src="games/legend of zelda.png" >}}

TL;DR: It's a miss for me, but mostly because of its age.

I picked this one up because I've had very fond memories of A Link to the Past and Link's Awakening (the later of which I played on the GBC as a child) and I was hoping to get another fun 2D Zelda experience.  While I imagine I would've like these games had I played them on release, playing them now left me wanting.  The biggest complaint is the fact that you can only have two items active at a single time (sword and shield included).  I understand why this was the case...this game was designed for the Game Boy Color, a system which only had two buttons other than the D-Pad.  However in practice I felt like I spent just as much time menuing my loadout as I did actually playing the game.

My other bit complaint was Oracle of Seasons specific, being the game that is more focused around combat vs Age's puzzles.  Honestly, I don't combat is the strong point in Zelda games, and an entire game with that at the forefront is unfortunately not that fun. I think I would've enjoyed Seasons better if I played it second and was able to take my linked equipment with me from Ages.

The linking mechanic was the main selling point of the game, and thankfully it does deliver.  Once you enter the second game (whichever one it is) you are constantly being given riddles that you have to take back to the first game to solve in order to get new powerful gear.  It's well implemented and a delight every time you solve one.  The linked story is basically non-existant, basically just a bossfight tacked onto the end of the game.

While this game was probably great for it's time, I didn't enjoy it in 2023.

## Ara Fell: Enhanced Edition

{{< figure src="games/ara fell.png" >}}

TL;DR:  Far from the best JRPG you'll ever play, but it's still quite good and worth a spot in your JRPG portfolio

Ara Fell is an indie JRPG, originally a JRPG maker game that was remade into Unity for the Enhanced Edition.  Nevertheless you can still feel the RPG Maker roots which may be a turn off for some people.

Personally, I really enjoyed Ara Fell.  The story has some traditional fantasy elements, but the world is still fairly unique and it takes those elements to new places.  I actually wouldn't be unhappy with a sequel to see what happens in the world next, although I'm happy to leave the ending open as well.

Mechanically the game does some great things that I haven't seen in JRPGS before.  Your HP and MP refill between every combat, which means I get to use my abilities to their full extent every combat, instead of saving my resources up for the boss battle at the end of the gauntlet. They also gave you only a few abilites per character, but that loadout was widely customizeable. 

## Freedom Planet 2

{{< figure src="games/freedom planet 2.png" >}}

TL;DR:  Mostly an improvement over the first, but I don't think I liked it as much.

The original Freedom Planet was a favorite game of mine, and so I was very execited when the sequel came out.  It ended up being a bit of letdown for me, but I think it's hugely because of my personal tastes and not much related to the game itself.

Generally, there are many small tweaks to the game that make it more of a fighter and less of a platformer.  There's the addition of a block & dodge mechanics, and the levels seem more layed out in a way to encourage exploration.  While the first game seemed like it wanted to be "Sonic with Better Combat" the sequel feels to me like it's trying to become its own thing, while also not wanting to let go of its Sonic roots.  For me, I much preferred the original Sonicesque design, although I think in general the sequel is probably more balanced.

My other critque of the game is its length.  While the original Freedom Planet had a similar length to Sonic, with only a handful of worlds with multiple acts per world, Freedom Planet 2 is easily 2 or 3 times as long (Steam reports my playtime at 12.6 hours), but visits each world only once.  If you want a large variety of levels, then this is probably only beneficial, but it comes at the cost of being memorable:  I can probably describe half of the Freedom Plent levels even without having played the game in years, whereas I couldn't tell you more than one of the Freedom Planet 2 ones.

One thing that is a strict upgrade in this game is the story.  The story was easily the weakest part of the first game, and while the new story isn't anything to write home about, it's at least serviceable.

If you haven't played the first one, probably just play that.  If you're looking for more of that, and you're okay with it being a little different, give Freedom Planet 2 a try.

## Deathloop

{{< figure src="games/deathloop.png" >}}

TL;DR: Not Arkane's strongest game, but still great and worth it for an Immersive Sim fan.

Deathloop is a heavily criticized game, but I think most of the criticisms were that people wanted the game to be something else instead of enjoying the game for what it was. It's not hitman, it's not thief, it's not prey. It's dishonored without the non lethal options.

While I was concerned with the repeatability of the content at first, in retrospect I think it was a really good design decision. This format allowed for a game without quick saves, whereas in other immersive sim games I tend to try again when things go south, this game forced me to deal with the consequences of my actions. And, I can always try again if a mission didn't go entirely as planned.

The game has a lot of detail put in to the levels. I've played quite a bit of this game and there's still crazy obscure things I've seen online that I had no idea were here. There's a lot of background story telling and the nonlinear nature of time in the game lets you discover the effects before the causes. The writing and voice acting is another standout. While the story itself is good-but-not-great, all the little character interactions in the game are amazing, especially the banter between Colt and Julianna.

Julianna invasions were very enjoyable. Given that the AI can be pretty simple, I could build my character around defending invasions and the cat and mouse aspect is really fun. I feel like the two characters are fairly balanced as well, with the defender rightly having a small advantage. Playing as the invader is fun too, although i wish the gear wasn't completely random... At the moment I've gotten the duel pistols 3 times but I still can't find a shotgun. That said, it's also probably good for the colt players as well, this way not every Julianna is a fully kitted out God.

Over all, well worth the money that I paid for it. Would recommend.

## Into The Breach

I personally wasn't interested in targeting my builds to meet achivements vs playing to win, and once I ran out of coins, I also ran out of patience with the game.

## Lightning Returns:  Final Fantasy XIII

{{< figure src="games/lightning returns.png" >}}

TL;DR: Mechanically enjoyable, stupid plot

Definitely a game that no one asked for, but it didn't turn out too bad! The main story is definitely the worst part...it took a story that already went up it's own ass in XIII-2, and continued the journey until I saw nose hairs. But, the performances are still good, and I got to see more of the characters I liked and fewer of the ones that I didn't like.  Vanille and Fang's returns were welcome, and the fact that I got to see less of Caius and Noel and none of Sarah also benefited the game.

I really liked the sidequests, and the fact that you get levels from questing and not from grinding...especially since grinding trash gives you more of another limted resource (time), I would've ended up way overleveled if I was also getting XP.  I didn't like the combat system at first, but by the end of the game I learned to really enjoy it.  I ended up clearing almost all side quests, including extincting everything except the two superbosses.

Overall, if you've made it through XIII-2, I don't see any reason you shouldn't continue onto this one.

## Kena: Bridge of Spirits

{{< figure src="games/kena.png" >}}

TL;DR: A delightful throwback to PS2-era platformers.

The final game I played this year, I went into Kena knowing it was well-recieved, but I didn't realize quite how much I would enjoy it.  Kena feels like Jak and Daxter, had Jak and Daxter been made in the 2020s.  The combat definitely draws inspiration from the Souls games, but adds in its own mechanics such as the bomb and the bow and arrow targeting of enemy weak spots.  Mechanically I loved this game and would love to play more.

One thing this game did great was also put copious amounts of side content around the game.  Between hats, mail, currency, challenges, and more, the game encourages you to fully explore every level and provides extra challenges for those who want it.

One thing this game falls short on is its story.  While I did enjoy the story of Kena, the game doesn't quite make you care about it quite enough.  The main character is a bit of a mystery, with only a little of her backstory being revealed throughout the story as well, so the player ends up feeling disconnected both from the plot of the game, but also the character representing them.  It does, however, leave room open for a sequel in which some of these questions are answered.

Overall a shockingly impressive first game from Ember Lab, and I hope they choose to make another game in the future.
