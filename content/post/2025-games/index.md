Well, I missed 2024, but here's my 2025 game roundup!

## Final Fantasy XIV: Dawntrail

{{< figure src="games/dawntrail.png" >}}

TL;DR Not quite as engaging as previous expansions.

While I don't quite dislike the story as much as some popular online discourse does, I do agree that there were a lot of missed opportunities here. This year, I stepped away a bit more from Final Fantasy XIV to do some other things. One of the big problems XIV has is a lack of content in the early days of the expansion:  When the expansion launches, unless you're a raider, there's not much else in the background to do!

Luckily they do come up with a lot more over the course of the year, and I do think the exploration zone and the field activities were very engaging this year. The normal raids themselves were also some peak XIV content, and I found myself looking forwards to the even-numbered patches so that I could start doing my weeklies again. The alliance raids in the off-patches didn't quite hold my attention as much, but I think it's really just not matching my interest, rather than an intrinsic problem to the raids.

## GregTech: New Horizons

{{< figure src="games/gtnh.png" >}}

TL;DR The factory must grow?

I've been playing this minecraft modpack in the same world on and off for years, so it's really no surprise that I put some more time into it this year. I managed to get a large chunk of the AE automation set up this time, really reducing the amount of effort it takes to generate materials in this game. I also rebuilt my ore processing line using multiblock structures, allowing me to process endgame ores significantly faster than before. I started and ended in IV. Once I get my last pieces of automation rebuilt, the next step is to finally rebuild my power generation to solve my myriad of energy problems..and then it's on to LuV!

Total play time: Uncountably high

## Dead Cells

{{< figure src="games/dead_cells.png" >}}

TL;DR yeah, I mean there's a reason it's a critically acclaimed roguelike game.

Dead Cells was actually a game I played for a bit when it first came out five years prior. Since then, however, a number of DLCs have come out featuring new paths, gear, etc, so I decided to give it another go...and it's a lot of fun!  I managed to make it through most of the bosses at a reasonable difficulty before deciding that some of the higher boss-cell difficulties were going to be a bit too grindy for my taste. The art is good, the music is bangin'...if "castlevania roguelike" sounds good to you, then this game should be at the top of your list.

Total play time (including the first time around): 90.6 hours

## Spyro Reignited Trilogy

{{< figure src="games/spyro.png" >}}

TL;DR I prefer the first one, but all three are excellent games.

Spyro is one of those games that I played the first 20 minutes of over and over again at a relative's house but never actually completed myself. However, my wife owns the remastered series, so I thought I'd give it a whirl and I quite enjoyed them!

The first game is probably my favorite of the three...it's very much "sonic-like" in nature, with an emphasis on finding the flow:  The levels are designed with the idea that you'll be charging through much of them, and are full of fun curves and jumps to take advantage of this. The Tree Tops are the best example of this, and a level in gaming that still sticks in my mind even at the end of the year.

Spyro 2 and 3 change up the theme a bit. If Sypro 1 is Sonic, then 2 and 3 are Mario. There's a greater emphasis on exploring the levels slowly and taking advantage of your moveset to uncover secrets. I didn't quite like that as much as the flow state that you get in for the first game, but they were all still enjoyable. (I 100%ed all titles, so I must have been having a great time!)

Total play time: 32 hours

## Subsurface Circular

{{< figure src="games/subsurface.png" >}}

TL;DR An engaging text-based adventure

At one point, I remembered that _Thomas Was Alone_ existed as one of my all time favorite games. After deciding to look up what the development studio had come out with since then, I noticed that among them were a pair of text-based adventures, the first of which was _Subsurface Circular_. In this game, you play as a detective robot who cannot leave his subway car due to his programming. You must interact with the other robots around you in order to solve a mystery.

Overall, I had a good time with this game. It's one of those games where your choices will have a minimal affect on the ending, only how you get there, but that never really bothered me. The actual gameplay mechanics are minimal...sometimes there's a small logic puzzle to solve, but mostly you can just click on dialog choices until the scene progresses. However, I found the world in which the game exists to be very interesting, and trying to discover more about it kept me playing the game to the end.

Total play time: 3.3 hours

## The Surge 2

{{< figure src="games/thesurge2.png" >}}

TL;DR Nonsense plot but fun gameplay

This was a DNF I had tried a few years back. This game is very unoptimized, and my computer at the time struggled to run it. Additionally, I had really enjoyed the mystery of the first game, and I was hoping for a more direct sequel, whereas this game starts you a significant amount of time after the first game.

In actuality, the game only takes place like a month after the first one, but in that month society somehow has moved from your normal post-capitalist dystopia to full air-conditioner worshiping wasteland, so I'm not sure if that timeline fully checks out. In general, the plot of this game is hot garbage, and only exists to string together cool levels and fight scenes. However, this game does excel at the soulslike gameplay formula they developed in the first game, and if you can tolerate the story, you'll have a great time. The bosses are fun, the levels are both interesting and do the fun dark-souls loopy thing. The bosses are intense and love to mess up your day.

## Paper Trail

{{< figure src="games/papertrail.png" >}}

TL;DR Checks all the indie puzzle game boxes.

This is a cute little puzzle game where your character moves across a piece of paper, and you can fold over the edges to reveal the back of the page, which is used to create new paths, connect items, and otherwise solve puzzles. The art is beautiful, and the story is at least passible enough to carry to the end of the game. This is an easy recommendation for anyone who might like this style of game.

Total play time: 4.5 hours

## Tales of Vesperia: Definitive Edition

{{< figure src="games/talesofvesperia.png" >}}

TL;DR DNF, not for me.

I tried with this one, I really did. The "Tales of" series is well known, and several of the games, including this one, are spoken of very highly. I had played Tales of Symphonia a few years back, and while I didn't think it was that good of a game, it was at least passible.

Vesperia though...this is one of the favorite games in the series, and I just can't get into it. I didn't like the Linear Motion Battle System in Symphonia and I didn't like it here either. The dialog is incredibly stilted and the plot did not hook me even a little bit. Given that this is one of the most popular games in the series, this is probably my exit from the "Tales of" series as well. There's too many other games out there to try.

Total play time: TODO

## System Shock (2023)

{{< figure src="games/systemshock.png" >}}

TL;DR Not the best first immersive sim for someone, but a good game for fans of the genre

Confession time:  I love immersive sims. I love System Shock 2, even with all of its flaws. Prey (2017) is one of my all-time favorite video games.

Additionally, I'm a big fan of Nightdive Studios. Not only do I love their mission of bringing old games back to life, I also think they're exceedingly good at it.

System Shock is their biggest undertaking yet, and I think they did an excellent job. I never played the original System Shock but it had a reputation an absolutely terrible UI, hindering the game's playability for those used to modern games.

I'm happy to say that Nightdive seemingly did an excellent job with this remake. Not everything is perfect, there's still some questionable design choices, but I think they did a great job walking the thin line of trying to make the original game playable while at the same time not making fans of the original game mad by changing too much. If I had one complaint, it's that the final boss encounter (new to the remake) is not very fun, but hey...it wouldn't be a System Shock game if the ending wasn't a letdown.

Total play time: 21.2 hours

## The Knight Witch

{{< figure src="games/knightwitch.png" >}}

TL;DR I enjoyed it, but it definitely favors very specific tastes and probably isn't for everyone.

The Knight Witch is a bullet hell metroidvania game, which is a weird blending of genres. It's not quite a bullet hell, as you have a pretty large hitbox, and a lot of the gameplay is about cover and using abilities to clear bullets vs the precise movement you usually find in the genre, but it still clearly has bullet hell bones. Die hard bullet hell fans will probably find this too different to enjoy, but if you're someone who finds both genres interesting, you'll probably find this quite enjoyable.

The plot doesn't bring much to the table. It's serviceable and carries you to the end of the story, but you probably won't think about it once you turn the game off. The art is cute, but again, nothing amazing. Overall, a fun experience to play once, and probably worth picking up at a decent discount.

Total play time: 12.6 hours

## Ape Out

{{< figure src="games/apeout.png" >}}

TL;DR Quick little beat-em up art piece.

I picked up Ape Out a long time ago as part of an EGS free game giveaway. It's a short little game where you play an ape who is breaking out of confinement (or possibility breaking into confinement based on the level). It has a very Hotline Miami kill-or-be-killed style of gameplay. The game itself is shown in a very minimalistic style, and has a fun jazz soundtrack that plays in the background while you destroy whatever environment you're in. It's a pretty lite and short game, but it also regularly goes down to $3 while on sale and definitely worth picking up at this price.

Total play time: 1.75 hours

## Platonic

{{< figure src="games/platonic.png" >}}

TL;DR Short Witness-like puzzle game that's worth playing

I'm honestly not sure how platonic ended up on my radar, but I'm glad it did!  Platonic is a environmental puzzle game, similar to The Witness where the solution to your puzzle is hidden in the environment around you. It's a lower-budget game with a pretty simple graphics style, but they do a lot to stretch what you're getting a long way. I found the difficulty level to be pretty solid--There were a few points where I had to look up what to do next, but for the most part I was able to solve the puzzles unassisted and feel smart while doing it.

Total play time: 4.7 hours

## Beatbuddy: Tale of the Guardians

{{< figure src="games/beatbuddy.png" >}}

TL;DR Probably not worth buying except for pennies, but worth playing if you already own it.

This was a short little adventure game where you're an underwater blob guy and you go on a 2D adventure where everything moves to a beat. Most of the gameplay is spent solving minor puzzles to unblock a path, or dodging obstacles to a beat. The game doesn't really do anything unique or interesting in 2025, but it's not hateful either!  It's just kind of a game that exists. I had picked this up in a humble bundle over a decade ago, and, while I wouldn't suggest paying for it, I had a good time playing it since I already owned it.

Total play time: 5.1 hours

## Monument Valley

{{< figure src="games/monumentvalley.png" >}}

TL;DR Simple puzzles, frustrating pace, beautiful world.

Monument Valley is a pretty well-known phone puzzle game. I have access to my wife's account through Steam Family Sharing and she owns this on PC for some reason so I thought I'd give it a shot!

The game is quite beautiful. It has a simplistic isometric style but the lighting is good and everything is quite pretty to look at. Solving the individual puzzles was fun, but my enjoyment was hampered by the slow movement speed of the character. This was almost certainly a deliberate choice in order to emphasize the casual feeling of the game. At the same time, it increased the time between "I know how to solve the puzzle" and "I've solved the puzzle" which is not the most enjoyable experience.

I will probably play the sequel anyway.

Total play time: 3.9 hours

## Triangle Strategy

{{< figure src="games/trianglestrategy.png" >}}

TL;DR I love this game and I'm not quite sure why.

As a big fan of Final Fantasy Tactics, this game has been on my radar since release. The game turned out to be an interesting mix of styles. There are of course tactics style missions, which I found to be incredibly fun. Each character in the game had a very specific style, and most of the very large cast each felt strong in their own way. I was able to have fun playing missions multiple times (a feature the game offers to gain more experience since the game doesn't feature random encounters).

From a strategy level, Triangle Strategy is pretty bare-bones, but I found I didn't miss it either. There's not a ton of character customization options...your choices are more "which character will you spend your resources on", and the only character specific choices are "which numerical buff will you unlock first". Usually though I often to just gravitate to "what are the best all around choices" for each character so for me removing the choice altogether was not a loss.

In between your combat are significantly long cutscenes in which you will make decisions determining the course of the campaign, giving the entire thing a very visual novel feel. This has been a turn off for many people, but I quite enjoyed the story. The main characters are all unique, and well voiced, and I'm a sucker for "fantasy political intrigue". The only thing that missed for me was the fact that your choices mean that you can't experience the entire game on a single playthrough, as I have too many other games I'm interested in to want to play through the entire campaign 3 times to see everything.

On that note, I should point out that this is technically a DNF for me. Because I wanted to go through this only a single time, I used the internet to find which choices lead to the "good ending" (referred to in guides as The Golden Path). Unfortunately when I got there, I discovered that the Golden Path is balanced for New Game Plus difficulty (I guess they didn't expect people to make the right choices by accident on the first go-round?), and put the game down rather than try and grind enough XP to beat out the final fights. Aside from that ending blip however, I had a very enjoyable time with this and would very much recommend it to fans of the genre.

Total play time: 30 hours

## Pseudoregalia

{{< figure src="games/pseudoregalia.png" >}}

TL;DR N64-style Metroidvania that has very strong and unique movement options

Pseudoregalia is another single-developer indie-darling, which first gained recognition due to it's low-poly "N64-style" graphics style. The game itself is a metroidvaina that avoids some of the traditional movement options (extra jumps, dash, etc) for a more unique set of wall-runs and kicks. Each action comes with trade-offs, leading to moveset that feels clunky at first, but as you learn the tradeoffs of each move during gameplay, eventually begins to feel surprisingly fluid and deep as you master each one.

Gameplay itself involves exploring a castle to find a number of mcguffins in order to unlock the final level. The story itself is pretty nonexistant, mostly appearing in the first and last cutscenes; the game prefers to focus on the meat of the gameplay instead. The levels themselves compliment the moveset well...while often time there is an obvious intended route, many alternate routes can be found as you stretch the capabilities of your moveset to the limit. There are also a number of challenges throughout the game, smartly with the most difficult challenges giving the least impactful rewards (letting the completion of the difficult challenge being a reward in itself).

While the game wasn't that long, I enjoyed it enough to 100% all of the challenges, and I'm eager to see what this developer does next.

Total play time: 10.1 hours

## Hades II

{{< figure src="games/hades2.png" >}}

TL;DR Better gameplay, worse everything else, still miles better than most other games.

I usually don't buy games on release (as should be obvious from this list) but I occasionally make exceptions for games that I'm very excited about. Hades II falls squarely in this list, as I've enjoyed everything Supergiant Games has ever made with Hades I being at the top.

Hades II ends up being an interesting successor to the original. On one hand, I think they did a great job changing the gameplay in a way that both distinguishes itself from the original and is also more enjoyable, while still being identifiable to the series. Melinoe is slower than Zagreus, creating a more deliberate gameplay style compared to the hit-and-run tactics of the first game. The addition of familiars and omega attacks add new depth to the choices that you're able to make as well, leading to a near endless number of viable playstyle.

That said, Supergiant is also traditionally known for their stories, and on this note Hades II missed the mark. The game can't decide if it wants to be the lighthearted family drama of the first game, or to have serious "You killed my father, prepare to die" stakes. While the patched ending helps a bit, I still feel the game suffers from tonal whiplash at some of the worst possible times. I also didn't feel connected to the side characters of this game in the same way I did in the first, I'm unsure if this is a writing issue or just my preferences.

Regardless, you'll spend most of your time blasting your way through the game's levels, and it's this point where the game shines anyway. I had enough of a good time that this game ended up being one of the few games I've 100%ed.

Total play time: 111.6 hours.

## Marvel's Spider-Man Remastered

{{< figure src="games/spiderman.png" >}}

TL;DR No open-world innovation, but delivers strongly on the Spider-Man fantasy.

This is a spider man game so strong that the best part of the game might actually be just traversing the city while heading to your next mission. I'm pretty sure during my entire playtime of this title I didn't use the fast travel system once, preferring instead of web-sling around the city, listening to the ranting of J. Jonah. Jameson over the airwaves. This game's Spider-Man fantasy also extends to its combat:  Spider-Man's moveset is full of classic maneuvers allowing you to use your webs and agility to your advantage and really feel like you're Spider-Man. While it does stick pretty strongly to the Arkham style of combat from which it clearly draws inspiration, the enemies in this game are at least aggressive enough on hard that you are forced to use kiting and crowd control to prevent yourself from being mobbed:  You're Spider-man, but you're not Superman.

The actual content of this game sticks pretty strongly to the Ubisoft open-world formula:  Clear a tower to unlock the map, then find collectables or clear challenges where ever they pop up. The game doesn't innovate at this point, but the content is at least decently fun, and I ended up 100%ing this game mostly because I was almost complete there by the time I finished the story.

The story itself is also decently fun. You get reimagined versions of a solid chunk of Spider-Man's rogue's gallery, and a lot of his supporting cast to boot. And without going into too many spoilers, a lot of the plot feels like a middle finger to One More Day, which is also fun to see.

The biggest miss of this game is a few sections where you play as either Mary-Jane or a not-yet-powered Miles Morales. These sections tend to be more stealth-focused and don't quite flow with the rest of the game. Overall though, I greatly enjoyed this game and look forward to eventually playing the sequels.

Total play time: 46.6 hours

## The Operator

{{< figure src="games/theoperator.png" >}}

TL;DR Short puzzle-solving narrative game with a controversial ending.

I first played this game as a Steam Next Fest demo last year, and I picked it up recently as it's not that expensive. This is a "you play as a guy facing a computer screen" game, where you solve crimes by connecting various dots between various databases, manuals, and other text and providing that information to agents in the field. The game quickly reveals that not everything is as it seems...

While short, I honestly had a blast with this game, as solving the various puzzles as presented to you did a great job of making you feel exceedingly smart as you connected the dots. The ending of the game was controversial for a lot of people, and, while I also admit it didn't quite hit for me, I also felt that it wasn't a bad ending either. Just be prepared if you play this game that you may or may not enjoy the end of the journey.

Total play time: 3 hours

## Quarantine Circular

{{< figure src="games/quarantinecircular.png" >}}

TL;DR Not as good as Subsurface, still worth playing

Another short text-based adventure, Quarantine Circular is the sequel to Subsurface Circular. This one finds you talking to an alien captured in the midst of a global pandemic. Rather than playing only one character, this adventure shifts your viewpoint between all sides of the conflict as the crew of a medical relief boat determine what to do with the alien they've captured.

There's less mystery involved in this one compared to Subsurface Circular, instead relying on your interest in seeing the story's resolution to drive you forward. Unlike Subsurface, this game contains multiple endings, although most of them are "early aborts" that occur if you chose particularly violent or uncompromising choices.

Overall, this one didn't grab my like it's predecessor did but it still makes it a fun short game and a good way to spend an evening

Total play time: 3 hours
